# Running Trust No One

Keep the organiser dashboard on the laptop and open Projector in a second tab
on the extended display. Use only the projector tab on the public screen.
It has no game-control permissions. Its QR opens name/PIN login for the event.

## Before cadets join

1. Create the single live event. It starts with an empty roster and joining closed.
2. Complete Setup’s three steps: date and name, group sizes/Traitor counts, then
   shields, chat and the supervised murder opportunity. Defaults are five groups
   of ten, two Traitors per group, shields off and the second murder supervised.
3. Edit the three mission cards. The supplied descriptions are starting points,
   not tested puzzles. Prepare the physical equipment, verified answers, scoring
   stages and private NCO notes. Mission points never decide deduction victories.
4. Check the Europe/London target timetable. Results finish by 21:10, clear up
   and form up run 21:10 to 21:15, and final parade is 21:15 to 21:30.
5. Have each NCO register a name/PIN account. Assign the account to its groups and
   choose permitted actions. The default NCO permissions cover timers, scoring,
   proxy votes, help, recovery, shields and supervised objectives. The main host
   controls public reveals unless those permissions are deliberately delegated.
6. Open joining and show the QR. Players create their own account, sign in and
   press Join the event. The server assigns numbers and balances group sizes.
   Public names initially use the player number. Approve a callsign in Players
   if wanted; account login names are not published to the projector automatically.

There is no event code and no emailed account. The player’s number identifies
their roster record; their private name/PIN login protects access. Do not project
PINs, accounts tables, recovery exports or the organiser dashboard.

## Roles

Close joining and check the roster. Choose Prepare random roles, or assign each
role manually from Players. Every group must have exactly its configured number
of Traitors and at least one Faithful. Prepared roles remain hidden from players.

To change an unreleased distribution, deliberately reset unreleased roles and
prepare again. Released roles cannot be rerolled. Release private envelopes only
after everyone is in the right group. Players press and hold to reveal; they can
hide the envelope and it also hides when the tab goes into the background.

Late arrivals register an account but cannot join automatically after roles are
prepared. The host may approve a late arrival into a group with space. After
release they enter as Faithful, with the existing roles preserved. Do not add
late players to a completed group. Explain this deliberate adjustment to staff.

## Phases and timers

Choose All groups, or one group when groups are at different stages. Start a
mission, discussion, voting, private planning, murder opportunity or finale using
the controls. Invalid transitions are rejected by the server, not just disabled
on the page. A command affecting several groups is atomic: an invalid group
prevents the whole action, so select that group individually if needed.

Timers share a server deadline. Pause, resume and extend update connected phones.
An expired countdown waits for an organiser and never publishes a result. The
target timetable does not automatically skip a late phase. Running late suggests
shortening a remaining mission, while protecting results, clear up and parade.

Use Check your device to publish a neutral announcement. The website cannot wake
a locked phone or appear over another app. During physical missions cadets can
put phones away until called back. Recent connection indicators are estimates,
not proof that somebody has left or should be eliminated.

## Voting

Start discussion, then open voting. Only active players may vote for another
active player in their own group. Changes replace the earlier vote until closure.
The player gets a receipt only after the server accepts it. NCO proxy votes need
a recorded reason and must obey the same eligibility rules.

Close voting explicitly. Missing votes become abstentions. No partial totals go
to players. A tie gets one new runoff ballot between tied candidates, followed
by an explicit host random draw only if the runoff is tied too. An empty ballot
must be reopened or skipped deliberately; nobody is randomly banished for zero votes.

Use Reveal outcome to publish the name, then Reveal role separately. A result
held privately is not an elimination until published. Repeated reveal requests
cannot eliminate somebody twice. Public information cannot meaningfully be made
secret again, even if an organiser later corrects a record.

## The two murder opportunities

Open at most two opportunities per group. The default first opportunity is
standard, and the second is the supervised objective during The Vault. Setup can
choose which opportunity is supervised or make both standard.

For standard selection, the main host can choose a designated deciding Traitor
using the decision-maker form. Quick controls use the first active Traitor by
player number. Active Traitors propose an eligible Faithful within their group.
If choices differ, the designated player decides; if eliminated, the remaining
active Traitor takes that role. No valid choice at closure means no murder.
The main host may instead record an eligible target using Manual murder decision,
with a reason. This is logged and still waits for a separate public reveal.

For a supervised objective, privately assign an eligible target and a physical
objective, such as getting them to say an agreed phrase. A completion report is
only a report. An authorised NCO must verify or reject it with a reason before
the deadline. Moving out of its mission expires an unconfirmed attempt as failure.

Lock or verify the outcome privately, then publish it at the scheduled reveal.
A pending victim remains publicly active and may vote. If that victim is banished
before the murder reveal, the murder is redundant and there is no replacement.
If the acting Traitor is banished after NCO confirmation, the pending outcome
still stands. Never announce a victim before publication.

If shields are enabled, an NCO records the owner and designated opportunity.
Only the owner and authorised organisers see ownership. The shield is checked
on the server, does not stop banishment, and never causes a replacement victim.

## Messages, help and recovery

Open a timed private planning window when appropriate. Ordinary dashboards show
a neutral prompt for everyone. Active Traitors see partners in their group and
can use preset messages. Short free text is optional and visible to authorised
organisers. NCOs can send private instructions and supervise group communication.
Eliminated Traitors immediately lose future messages, objectives and actions.

Need help creates one outstanding request, with repeated-tap protection. The
assigned NCO acknowledges it. From Players, an authorised organiser can replace
a player’s four-digit PIN and revoke old sessions while preserving their number,
group, role and submitted votes. Verify the person physically before recovery.
Never ask them to announce the new PIN to the group.

## Results and fallback

After three banishments, surviving Traitors win their group; otherwise Faithful
win. Catching all Traitors earlier closes that group’s deduction after confirmed
pending outcomes are handled. There is no parity victory, recruitment or extra
role. Everyone may still help with missions. Resolve outstanding decisions before
the finale and final reveal.

Download a private recovery record before the event and at major breaks. Keep it
on the organiser device, away from the projector. Print player-number slips from
Players if useful. The slips do not expose PINs. Follow PAPER-FALLBACK.md if the
connection fails. Use REHEARSAL.md before relying on the website at the event.
